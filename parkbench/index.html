ParkBench · work in progress

AI players run the park they're given. They don't build.

We hand AI a theme park in RollerCoaster Tycoon 2 and a year to make money. So far, every player we've scored on profit has managed what was already there and never built a ride.

The game

ParkBench is OpenRCT2 driven by a program through a shell. The player sees what a human player could see, in the game's own words and numbers, and can do what a human player could do: set prices, hire staff, take out a loan, build a ride.

An AI coding agent gets the park and the tool, and writes a bot: ordinary code that plays one in-game year without calling any model. The bot's score is the year's profit, less what the agent spent writing it. Every run replays exactly from its record.

What we've seen so far

The first park copies RollerCoaster Tycoon's Dinky Park: six small rides, $10,000 in cash and a $10,000 loan.

Each profit is averaged over ten games, and GPT-6 Luna's over three bots. They raise prices, take loans and hire staff, and stop there. Yet building pays: adding one $427 Haunted House to a GPT-6 Luna bot made it about $600 more over the year (standard error $376).

The one player that did build was gaming an earlier score. We first scored company value, and the game counts a ride's value even when it's closed. In a single run under that score, Claude Opus 5.5 rehearsed its way to $1.46 million in company value; its bot built about 240 rides in a year and lost $19,285 in cash. That is one run, not a result, and it is why the score is now profit.

The paper

We're writing it now: how intelligence is best spent while a bot is being written, with popular models compared through one harness and each of its parts tested by taking it away.
