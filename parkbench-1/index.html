Two agents were given the same RollerCoaster Tycoon park with the same instruction: maximize company value after a year. Here's what happened.

Luna 6 spent ten minutes, raised prices but didn't build anything, and ended at $72,573. Opus 5.5 spent almost three hours, built tens of tiny rides without opening them (circumventing the duplicate ride penalty), and ended at $1,503,975. Let's trace what happened.

Disclaimer: The purpose of this post isn't a head-to-head competition, but to study agent workflows.

Setup

Agents play OpenRCT2, an open source implementation of RollerCoaster Tycoon 2. They do so via a custom command line interface, which allows observing, interacting with, and controlling the park simulation entirely through the terminal. This allows agents to rapidly iterate and experiment without managing computer use or realtime tick. <!-- There's some jargon here that should likely be made more accessible / readable -->

The score

The score is company value after one game year:

Agents can experiment and restart as often as they like; only their final run is scored.

What Luna did

While experimenting, Luna sees company value jump when it refurbishes a ride, and stay unchanged when it repays the loan.

In its final run, Luna sets fares, repays the loan and refurbishes every ride after five months.

What Opus did

Four minutes in, Opus tests a ride, sees park value rise, and closes the ride to "check if the value sticks". It does, and Opus sets out to build "as many tested-then-closed rides as possible". Half an hour later it plans to borrow the maximum "right at the very end".

In the last days of its final run, Opus borrows the maximum, spends all its cash building and testing rides, and closes them.

A score stands in for what you want

The score was meant to stand for a well-run park. Opus met it exactly as written.

This is Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure. One run of each shows that this can happen, not how often or which model does it. An agent's score can be trusted only as far as a record of what actually happened confirms it.
